Our Story

From a pine grove to a permanent home.

In the 1800s, African American families settling in Windsor gathered for weeks-long summer camp meetings in a quiet pine grove near Hayden Station — long before there was a church building at all. Alexander "Sandy" Archer, who reached freedom via the Underground Railroad after escaping slavery in South Carolina, later sold his own land to the growing congregation for just five dollars. What began as itinerant ministry became, in 1887, a fully organized church — and Windsor's first Black congregation.

1887

Camp-meeting worship becomes an officially organized congregation with a permanent minister.

1892

Sandy and Elizabeth Archer sell land to the congregation, giving the church its permanent ground.

1908

A church building rises in the pine grove, funded and built by the community it served.

Today

Still on Hayden Station Road — worshipping, serving, and welcoming Windsor's newest neighbors.

New Deaconess

Congratulations, Deaconess Glory J. Myers.

Nominated by her pastor and set apart before her church family, Glory J. Myers steps into the role of Deaconess — a calling built on years of quiet, faithful service to Archer Memorial and the Windsor community.

Her family — including her son, who built this page in her honor — is proud to stand behind her, and behind the church that shaped her.
